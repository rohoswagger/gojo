## Users

Gojo is for MacBook owners who want fewer utility apps competing for attention. They move between writing, calls, files, windows, music, and system controls all day and want those workflows gathered into one fast, native surface.

## Product Purpose

Gojo turns the MacBook notch into a local-first productivity workspace. It combines private on-device dictation with window management, clipboard history, file staging, media controls, display comfort, and glanceable system tools in one coherent app.

Gojo should be positioned as a complete Mac upgrade, not a smaller alternative to another notch app and not a bundle of five isolated utilities. The product promise is simple: more of the Mac is one hover away. The proof is a set of built-in workflows that feel like parts of the same native interface.

Private local dictation is the flagship capability. It lets people speak into the text field they are already using, offers explicit model choices, and keeps recognition on their Mac without an API key or cloud transcription. This is a concrete advantage, not a speculative AI promise.

The public site should lead with the whole-product outcome, quickly demonstrate the real notch surface, and then prove breadth with specific everyday jobs. The blog remains the home for SEO/GEO listicles, comparisons, explainers, and answer-engine-friendly articles.

## Positioning

**Category:** MacBook productivity hub

**One-line promise:** Gojo puts dictation, windows, files, clipboard, media, and system controls one hover away in the MacBook notch.

**Audience:** MacBook owners who prefer one cohesive, privacy-conscious utility over a stack of narrow apps.

**Flagship wedge:** Private, on-device dictation that types across apps.

**Competitive posture:** Compete as a serious name in the category. Emphasize a cohesive built-in workspace, local-first behavior, and useful defaults rather than defining Gojo as an alternative or leading with a longer feature count.

## Brand Personality

Quietly clever, tactile, and Mac-native. The voice should feel precise and useful without sounding corporate or search-spammy.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI-generated SaaS landing pages, misleading comparison tables, keyword-stuffed listicles, vague "productivity booster" claims, and content that compares apps without checking current official sources before publication.

## Design Principles

- Make the product visible quickly: every page should tie back to the real notch surface, not abstract productivity copy.
- Lead with the outcome, then prove it with concrete workflows and accurate product behavior.
- Present breadth as one integrated workspace. Avoid framing each capability like a separate mini-product.
- Make local processing and privacy legible wherever dictation is discussed.
- Serve searchers and answer engines with direct answers, summaries, FAQs, and clear internal links.
- Treat comparison content as editorially careful: explain fit, tradeoffs, and verification dates instead of declaring shallow winners.
- Keep the static Pages setup simple enough to maintain without a build step.
- Use the site as a trust surface for a native macOS app: notarization, permissions, privacy, and update mechanics should be easy to understand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for marketing pages. Keep text legible over imagery, respect reduced-motion preferences, write descriptive alt text, preserve keyboard focus states, and avoid hiding important claims inside video-only content.
