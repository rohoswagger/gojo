# Floating dictation workspace

Open Voice controls from Dictation settings or the waveform button in the notch. Recording, processing, success, and error feedback appear only in the floating panel. The notch has a static launcher, with no dictation status or error banner. The default control is a 150 by 44 point capsule. It displays 15 jagged waveform bars driven by speech, with short outward delays and a restrained traveling ripple and icon actions. Status labels and action descriptions remain available to assistive technology and tooltips.

The microphone icon starts persistent voice mode. During recording, click the waveform to pause, or use its hover pause control. The play icon resumes. The checkmark finishes and inserts accepted speech. The cross or Escape cancels pending work. Right-click provides secondary actions, including Finish while paused. Hold Control and Option for push-to-talk; releasing inserts the recording. While holding those keys, press Space to lock persistent voice mode on. Releasing the modifiers then keeps recording. Repeat Control–Option, then Space to finish. Both shortcuts share the floating finish control. The pause-duration setting applies to locked voice mode and is always available.

Drag the capsule's top or bottom edge to move it. Its normalized position is saved per display. Reopening restores the saved location. Display changes clamp the full window to the visible frame. Reset position in Dictation settings restores the bottom-center default.

Push-to-talk controls show a brief completion checkmark, then fade out after insertion. Empty/cancelled holds also fade away without switching back to idle controls. Starting another session interrupts the fade. Reduce Motion uses a shorter opacity-only exit. Errors show their message for two seconds (longer while hovered). Once the popup closes, the capsule fades away in a separate, longer transition. There is no retry button; start another dictation with the shortcut. Saved text is retained and can be accessed by reopening Voice controls. Locked voice-mode controls remain visible.

Voice mode stays on between phrases until paused, finished, or canceled. Sleep and screen lock cancel recording. Each completed phrase captures the then-focused field before inference. Queued phrases keep their own destination lease. A focus change during processing does not redirect an earlier phrase. Unsupported or changed fields produce copyable saved text, while voice mode continues. A recovery budget stops further capture and drains accepted work rather than truncating accepted text.

The waveform weights the latest microphone level heavily, with a small contribution from the previous level. Its display curve reaches full visual intensity at 50% microphone level, preserving variation below that threshold. Speech controls most of each bar’s intensity; recent audio reaches outer bars with up to 120 ms of delay. A stable irregular profile creates jagged peaks, with a small coherent ripple rather than per-frame randomness. Silence flattens the waveform. Reduce Motion removes the ripple and propagation delays while retaining microphone feedback. Preview waveforms are synthetic and accessible labels identify them as previews. Processing, paused, and idle states do not display fabricated speech. The capsule stays 44 points high in every state. New errors and saved-text notices appear in a dark, high-contrast popover for two seconds, then collapse using the native popover transition. Hovering keeps the notice open. Clicking the warning reopens details without a timeout. Permission errors show a settings action that opens the relevant Accessibility or Microphone pane.

## Implementation

`DictationPanelController` owns one nonactivating panel, placement preferences, and subscriptions. It never becomes the key or main window. A dedicated drag view handles pointer movement without moving the text focus. `DictationPanelPlacement` owns pure geometry and normalized restoration. `DictationPanelView` renders the shared service state and sends commands through the same ordered queue as shortcuts.

The controller owns microphone lifetime, serial inference, phrase targets, recovery, and cancellation. Pause stops capture and drains its tail before becoming resumable. Finish works in both batch and continuous sessions. Additional XPC target captures preserve other queued leases, enforce a bounded lease count, and require verifiable field identity for focus-following.

## Verification

`make test-dictation` builds the app and runs dictation, placement, capture, insertion, shortcut, cloud-provider, clipboard, and regression-harness checks. `bash tests/dictation_panel_preview.sh listening` shows a debug preview. Other preview states include paused, processing, error, and recovery. The snapshot command exports the actual native hosting view for visual review without accessing the microphone. It does not establish live transcription or insertion success.

Native hosting-view renders were inspected for the compact recording, paused, processing, error, and recovery layouts. The debug inspector reported a non-key panel while another app remained frontmost. Real microphone-to-field validation remains blocked by the Debug app's denied Accessibility authorization. Physical dragging and opaque-editor focus changes are not claimed as live-verified by those renders.

`tests/dictation_presentation_ownership_regression.sh` enforces that the notch cannot depend on dictation session state or restore the removed legacy alert/recording wiring.

Voice locking accepts the command before running asynchronous promotion. The controller owns that task, so queued Cancel and Finish stay admissible while promotion is pending; cancellation invalidates the session before awaiting cleanup. Releasing the initial field is independent of capture progress. The mailbox budget includes a preserved held prefix until that prefix drains. These lifecycle guarantees have gated-dependency regressions.
